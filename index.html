Infineon Adapts Car and Factory Chips to Make Humanoid Robots Safer
German chipmaker Infineon is adapting its automotive and industrial chips for humanoid robots, Digitimes reported on 24 November. The focus is functional safety, dexterity and power efficiency. Infineon wants to use the safety systems it builds for cars to stop a robot's AI from turning a bad decision into a dangerous movement. It has shown a board that combines a controller, gallium nitride (GaN) power chips and position sensors to drive motors of up to 1.2 kW, and GaN could shrink the electronics needed for each joint. Infineon estimates there's about US$500 of semiconductors in each humanoid robot. It hasn't given a production timeline, saying volumes will follow demand.

🔬 Science
🔭 SPACE
Euclid Telescope Finds the Most Distant Quasar Yet, From 630 Million Years After the Big Bang
Europe's Euclid space telescope has found the most distant quasar known, a blazing object powered by a giant black hole. Called J1504+6252 and nicknamed Dún Aonghasa, its light set out about 630 million years after the Big Bang, at a redshift of 8.13. That beats Euclid's own record from July, a quasar at redshift 7.77, by about 40 million years. Follow-up observations with the Hubble, Webb and Keck telescopes and the Large Binocular Telescope confirmed it. The black hole at its centre is about 360 million times the mass of the Sun. Astronomers aren't sure how something that big grew so fast when the universe was less than 5% of its current age. The results are published in Astronomy & Astrophysics, and ESA announced them on 24 November.
💡 Business Insight
Before an AI Agent Spends Your Money, Write Down Its Rules and Its Limit
This week Walmart let an AI agent buy goods for shoppers within a set spending limit, and Anthropic released agents that approve work against a written policy. Both work the same way: the AI acts on its own, but only inside rules a person has written down. Suppliers and banks will offer the same thing to small businesses soon, so it's worth deciding your rules now. Open ChatGPT, Claude or Copilot and ask it to help you write a one-page purchasing policy: which suppliers you use, what you normally order, the most any single order can cost without your sign-off, and which purchases always need a person, like new equipment or anything for a specific job. Keep a copy for your office staff too. It's useful today for anyone ordering on the business card, and when an AI ordering tool does turn up, you'll already have the limits ready to set.
