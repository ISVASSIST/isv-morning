💡 Business Insight
Wholesale Power Just Got 60% Cheaper in Victoria — Has Your Trade Business's Retail Plan Caught Up?
AEMO confirmed this week that wholesale electricity prices in Victoria fell 60% last quarter as wind generation ramps up — but wholesale savings rarely flow through to a small business's retail contract automatically, and compressors, blast pots, dust extraction and workshop plant are some of the most electricity-hungry gear on any trades premises. Most retail energy plans lock in a rate months in advance and simply don't move when the wholesale market does. This is a genuinely useful, low-effort job for AI: photograph or upload your last two energy bills and ask an AI assistant to extract your current rate, usage pattern and contract end date, then compare it against current retail offers for your postcode. For a workshop running compressors and extraction most of the day, even a few cents per kWh adds up fast over a financial year.
🧠 Things Worth Knowing
◆The UK banned silica sand for abrasive blasting all the way back in 1949 under the Silicosis and Asbestos Regulations — decades ahead of most of the world on the silicosis risk, though the legal definition of 'sand' stayed loose enough to need closing again with the 1999 COSHH update.
◆Steel pipelines and ship hulls are often protected not by paint alone but by bolting on a block of zinc or magnesium — a sacrificial anode. Because zinc corrodes before steel does, it 'sacrifices' itself and generates a small protective current that keeps the steel from rusting until the anode itself wears out and needs replacing.
◆The Antikythera mechanism, recovered from a Roman-era shipwreck off Greece in 1901, is a 2,000-year-old bronze mechanical computer with over 30 surviving gearwheels that could predict eclipses and calculate Olympic Games dates — a level of engineering not replicated again for roughly 1,000 years.
