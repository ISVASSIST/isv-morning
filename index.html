🔬 Science
🌊 OCEANS · AIR QUALITY
The Ocean's Natural Iodine Won't Clean Up Future Air Pollution, Study Finds
The ocean releases iodine into the air, where it helps break down ground-level ozone, a pollutant that harms lungs and crops and traps heat. Scientists had hoped rising iodine emissions might help offset future pollution. A new study from the University of York and the University of East Anglia, published in Science Advances, finds the opposite: global iodine emissions should stay roughly flat through the 21st century, and their air-cleaning and cooling effects will stall and weaken slightly by 2100. Professor Lucy Carpenter says warmer seas change the chemistry at the ocean's surface and slow how fast iodine escapes into the air. The paper also covers changes in the Arctic as sea ice melts. The upshot: cutting pollution at the source will matter more, because nature won't do it for us.
💡 Business Insight
Electricity Is Up 25%: Let AI Read a Year of Power Bills and Find What Is Driving Yours
Wednesday's inflation figures showed electricity up 25.2% over the year now the government rebates have ended, and in a workshop with compressors, extraction fans, ovens and welders, that hits hard. Most owners pay the bill without reading it. Instead, download the last 12 months of bills as PDFs (and your interval data from your retailer or distributor if you can get it) and upload them to ChatGPT, Claude or Copilot. Ask it to set out your usage, peak and off-peak rates, demand charges and daily supply charge month by month, show which months and times cost the most, and estimate what you'd save by running the big compressor or curing oven outside peak times, fixing air leaks or moving to a different tariff. Then ask it to draft an email to your retailer asking for a better offer. Check its numbers against the bill before you act, but an hour's work could take a real chunk out of a bill that has just gone up by a quarter.
