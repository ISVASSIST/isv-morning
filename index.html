World Court Finds Myanmar Committed Genocide Against the Rohingya
The International Court of Justice in The Hague ruled on Thursday that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya during its military's 2016-2017 "clearance operations". Human Rights Watch says it's the first time the court has found a country responsible for committing genocide. The judges voted nine to six. The court also found Myanmar failed to prevent and punish genocide, and ordered it to investigate and prosecute those responsible, including military commanders, and to compensate victims. Myanmar has two years to report back. The ruling is final and binding, but the court has no way to enforce it, so rights groups are now calling for sanctions.

📋 RED TAPE
Productivity Commission Calls for Less Business Reporting
The Productivity Commission's interim report on business reporting, released this week, says some non-financial reporting rules should be scaled back or redesigned to cut compliance costs. One example is the scheme that makes large businesses report twice a year on how fast they pay small suppliers. The commission found payment times have improved only slightly since it began. It also warned that extending climate reporting to medium-sized businesses would add a big compliance load. For small trades, the payment-times finding matters most. Don't assume big customers will pay on time just because they have to report it.

🔬 Science
🌊 DEEP SEA
A Strange Bacterium Lives Beneath the Skin of Deep-Sea Sea Pigs
Sea pigs are small, plump relatives of sea cucumbers that walk along the deep ocean floor. University of Tokyo researchers have found a new bacterium living in the layers beneath their skin. In some samples it made up more than 70% of all the bacterial DNA found there. They've named it Candidatus Abyssoplasma scotoplanesicola. Its genome is tiny, and it seems to rely on its host for amino acids, fats and minerals. What the sea pig gets in return is still a mystery. The study is in ISME Host Microbe.

Before You Take On a Big New Customer, Ask AI to Check Their Credit Signals
The Productivity Commission found big businesses are paying small suppliers only a little faster than before. A large new account can look great on paper and still leave you waiting 60 days or more. Before you quote a big job for a customer you haven't worked with, ask ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini to research them. Get it to look for recent news, any administration or liquidation notices, court cases, reviews from other subcontractors and their published payment terms. Then ask it to sum up the risk in plain English and suggest sensible terms, like a deposit or progress payments. It takes about ten minutes.
