Deadly Virus Linked to an 83% Drop in Tree Frog Breeding in a Brazilian Rainforest Reserve
A study in Biodiversity and Conservation tracked monkey frogs at a pond in Santa Catarina, in Brazil's Atlantic Forest. It linked repeated outbreaks of ranavirus, a disease that kills frogs, fish and reptiles, to an 83% drop in the frogs' breeding there. Ranavirus has caused frog die-offs on several continents. The researchers say it shows that disease can quietly wipe out frogs even inside protected reserves, so monitoring needs to look for infection as well as counting animals.
