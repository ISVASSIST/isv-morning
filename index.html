🔬 Science
🔥 ENERGY
Salt in the Air Decides Whether a Hydrogen Flame Can Be Seen
Hydrogen flames are almost invisible, which is a safety problem for hydrogen in homes and industry. Researchers from Adelaide University, UNSW and Gas Infrastructure Research Australia found that the orange glow they sometimes show comes mainly from sodium in the air, often sea salt blown inland. In outdoor tests near Adelaide, the sodium signal varied by more than 28 times between the lowest and highest readings with the burner unchanged. The study, in Next Energy, says safety systems should rely on UV, infrared or heat sensors rather than flame colour.
💡 Business Insight
Before You Sell or Trade the Old Ute, Ask AI to Price It Against Live Listings
Used vehicle values are falling, and dealers know it. Before you trade in or sell a work ute or van, copy 10 to 15 current listings for the same make, model and year range from carsales or Gumtree into ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini. Add your own vehicle's kilometres, service history, fit-out and any damage. Ask it to rank the comparable listings, estimate a realistic private sale price and a likely trade-in price, and list what's pushing your value up or down. Then ask it to draft the sale ad. Check the numbers yourself before you accept an offer, but you'll walk into the dealership knowing what the vehicle is worth.
