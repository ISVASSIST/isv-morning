🔬 Science
🧠 NEUROSCIENCE
Brain Scans May Flag Alzheimer's Risk Years Before Memory Problems Appear
A Stanford-led team published in Science Advances has built a brain-connectivity score from resting-state brain scans. In older adults whose memory tests looked normal, the score separated people into 'SuperAger-like' and 'Alzheimer-like' groups. The researchers say it predicted risk as well as or better than existing biomarkers like amyloid and tau, and earlier. It worked best combined with them. Reports say it predicted who developed the disease within about 5 to 12 years. It's still a research tool, not a clinical test yet.
💡 Business Insight
Let AI Turn a Coating Datasheet Into a One-Page Cheat Sheet for the Crew
Coating datasheets run to several pages, and the numbers that matter on site are buried in them: minimum and maximum application temperature, humidity limits, how far above the dew point the steel must be, recoat windows, pot life and full cure time. Upload the manufacturer's PDF into ChatGPT, Claude or Copilot and ask for a one-page table of those figures in plain English, with the warm and cold-weather limits up front. Check every number against the original before it goes in the van, then laminate it or put it in the job folder. In a week like this, with dry days followed by a 25°C Thursday and possible storms, the crew can check the recoat window on the spot without phoning the office.
