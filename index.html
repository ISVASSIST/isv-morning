🔬 Science
🌏 EARTH SCIENCE
Scientists Explain How a Big Quake Can Set Off Small Ones Thousands of Kilometres Away
Big earthquakes sometimes trigger smaller ones on faults thousands of kilometres away, and it's never been clear why the passing waves are strong enough. Engineers Chao Huang and Jun Yang at the University of Hong Kong think they've found the answer. Their simulations, published in Science Advances, show the waves squeeze the crushed rock inside a fault and push up the water pressure in it. In their model, that pressure reached about 1.7 MPa, roughly 10 times the direct push from the waves, which weakens the fault enough to slip. The water drains slowly, so the fault can stay weak after the shaking stops. It also helps explain why rolling Rayleigh waves trigger quakes better than side-to-side Love waves. The results come from computer models, so lab tests on real fault rock are still needed. Phys.org covered it on 23 November.
💡 Business Insight
Before the Slowdown Bites, Ask AI How Much of Your Work Comes From Your Top Three Customers
Yesterday's figures showed business activity shrinking at its fastest pace in five years, so now is a good time to check how exposed you are. Export the last 12 months of invoices from Xero or MYOB as a spreadsheet, take out bank details and anything private, and upload it to ChatGPT, Claude or Copilot. Ask it to group revenue by customer and by industry, and show what share your top three customers make up. If one or two clients bring in more than about a third of your work, or most of it comes from a single sector like construction or manufacturing, you're more exposed if that sector slows. Then ask the AI to list the customers who used you once or twice and haven't been back, and draft a short, friendly check-in for each. It takes about 20 minutes and gives you a clear list of where to look for work before things get quiet.
