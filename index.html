Scientists Find a Hidden Cause of High Blood Pressure That Routine Tests Can Miss
A UK-led team fitted 60 patients with a waist-worn device called U-RHYTHM, sampling fluid beneath the skin every 20 minutes for 24 hours, and found bursts of hormone production overnight — while asleep — in people with a condition called primary aldosteronism, which affects up to one in five people with high blood pressure and is rarely caught by a single daytime blood test.
