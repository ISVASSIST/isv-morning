The Payroll Change Nobody Is Talking About — Payday Super Starts July 1 and It Changes Your Cash Flow Permanently
For 34 years, Australian employers have paid superannuation quarterly — a known, manageable lump sum four times a year. From 1 July 2026, that changes permanently. Under the new Payday Super rules, super must be paid within seven business days of every single pay run. For a trades business paying staff weekly, super is now a weekly cash flow item. For fortnightly payroll, it is fortnightly. Quarterly budgeting for super is over. The immediate practical steps: first, check whether your payroll software — Xero, MYOB, Employment Hero, or similar — is Payday Super compliant. Most providers have been rolling out updates, but you need to confirm yours is live, not planned. Second, if you currently use the ATO's Small Business Super Clearing House, stop: it closes permanently on 1 July. Register with an alternative clearing house — Beam, QuickSuper, or your super fund's own portal — before June 30. Third, make sure your final quarterly super payment for this financial year reaches employees' funds before June 30 if you want to claim the deduction this year. The businesses that handle this quietly are the ones whose payroll software is already set up. The ones who find out in July are the ones who cop late-payment penalties from day one of the new financial year. Eleven days. Check the payroll software today.

Introduced by the Keating Government in 1992 at a 3% contribution rate, the Super Guarantee reaches its legislated ceiling of 12% on 1 July 2026. The system has accumulated over $3.9 trillion in assets — making Australia the fourth-largest pension pool on Earth and larger than the country's entire annual GDP.
◆The FIFA World Cup Trophy, introduced in 1974 after the original Jules Rimet Trophy was permanently stolen in Brazil in 1983, is crafted from solid 18-carat gold with a malachite base and weighs 6.175 kilograms. Under FIFA rules, no winning nation may ever permanently keep the original — each receives a gold-plated replica. It was designed by Italian sculptor Silvio Gazzaniga. The Jules Rimet was famously stolen from a London exhibition in 1966, recovered four days later by a dog named Pickles, then lost forever after Brazil was gifted permanent custody in 1970.
