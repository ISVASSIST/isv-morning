💡 Business Insight
Canberra's New 'Right to Erasure' Privacy Law Is Aimed at Big Tech — But the Habit It's Asking For Applies to You Too
The draft privacy reforms unveiled yesterday technically only bite platforms with $500 million-plus in revenue, so no trades business needs to panic about compliance. But the 'right to erasure' reflects where community expectations are heading on personal data, and most small operators are sitting on more of it than they realise — customer phone numbers and addresses in a phone, before-and-after job photos with someone's house in the background, quotes with names and details saved in an AI tool. It costs nothing to start being deliberate about it now: know what you're storing, delete what you don't need, and ask any AI tool you use where customer data actually goes. Getting ahead of a habit is a lot cheaper than retrofitting one after a law forces the issue.
