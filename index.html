🔬 Science
📡 WEATHER · TELECOMMUNICATIONS
Future 5G Interference Could Throw Off Forecasts for Severe Weather, Study Finds
A study in Geophysical Research Letters, led by Behzad Golparvar of Rutgers University, has found that signals leaking from future 5G networks could interfere with the satellites used to forecast severe weather. Weather satellites measure water vapour at about 23.8 GHz, very close to the 24.25-27.5 GHz band some 5G networks use. The team simulated a 2040 scenario using Hurricane Ida. The interference shifted the satellite readings by up to 3.75 degrees, which changed the forecast rainfall by up to 10 mm and temperatures by more than 2°C, with the effects spreading offshore. The authors say longer tests over many storms are needed to show how much everyday forecasts would actually suffer. It's a timely one on a weekend when Melbourne is relying on accurate storm forecasts.

Your AI Allowance Just Got Smaller: Match the Model to the Job
OpenAI's billing fix means ChatGPT users will hit their limits on the top GPT-6.2 models up to 40% sooner. Every AI provider rations its best models, so it pays to use them deliberately. Sort your everyday AI jobs into two piles. Quick tasks go on the fast, cheaper model: rewording a text to a customer, summarising an email chain, turning job notes into a tidy list. Save the top model for the jobs where accuracy matters: pricing a complex quote, checking a coating specification against a datasheet, reviewing a contract or a safe work method statement. Keep one shared document of prompts that work, so nobody burns their allowance re-explaining the same job every morning. Once a month, check your usage page. If nobody's hitting the limit, you may be paying for a bigger plan than you need, and if one person always is, upgrade just that seat rather than the whole team.
